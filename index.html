It follows the game

Entry flows with the play

Every play type opens a ladder — each answer reveals only the next question that can follow it. Pick Pass and you are asked completed, sacked or intercepted; pick completed and the passer and receiver pickers appear, already ordered by who has been doing it tonight. A touchdown offers the try, not a punt. Fourth down offers the punt and the field goal. After a change of possession the sides swap themselves.

And a rung can be answered whichever way you saw it. Enter the yards, or enter the yard line he was tackled on and StatChat works out the gain. Nothing that cannot happen on this down is ever on the screen.

It learns as it goes

StatChat remembers who does what

Once a quarterback has thrown, he is pre-selected on the next pass. The same goes for the running back, the receiver, the punter, the placekicker, the kickoff specialist and the returner — every position StatChat logs. Each is remembered separately, because the man who kicks off is often not the man who kicks the extra point.

The picker reorders itself as the game goes on. Anyone who has already carried, caught or made a tackle sorts to the front, ahead of the rest of the roster and ranked by what they have actually done tonight — so by the second quarter your top backs, receivers and tacklers are the first names on the screen. Every suggestion is only a suggestion: tap to confirm, or pick anyone else.

Built on NFHS rules, not adapted from someone else's

    
Automatic first down under NFHS belongs to four fouls, not the dozen the NFL awards
       it to. Pass interference is fifteen yards with no automatic first down, not the NFL's
       spot foul. StatChat gets these right because it was built for high-school football
       specifically — so the stat sheet you hand a coach matches the rule book the officials
       are using.
