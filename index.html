to entering plays

No complicated setup, no install, no training class. Make an account, build a game, and you are scoring — inside the time it takes the teams to finish warm-ups.

Create a game in three steps

01

Game details

Opponent, date, home or away, quarter length. Thirty seconds.

02

Rosters and starters

Name your team's likely starters and upload the opponent's roster. Yours carries over, so week two is a glance rather than a rebuild.

03

Opponent logo and colors

Add their logo and two colors, and every graphic picks them up. Nothing else to configure.

Then a few taps per play

Pick, fill, review — that is the shape of every play, whether it is a kneel or a fumble recovered three laterals later. The person scoring is usually also watching the game, minding a clock and answering someone, and the interface assumes that.

It follows the game

Entry flows with the play

The next question is the one the game just asked. A touchdown offers the try, not a punt. Fourth down offers the punt and the field goal. After a change of possession the sides swap themselves. Nothing that cannot happen on this down is on the screen, so there is less to read and less to get wrong.

It learns as it goes

It remembers who does what

Once a quarterback has thrown, he is pre-selected on the next pass. Same for the punter, the placekicker and the kickoff specialist — each remembered separately, because the man who kicks off is often not the man who kicks the extra point.

The picker reorders itself as the game goes on. Anyone who has already carried, caught or made a tackle sorts to the front, ahead of the rest of the roster and ranked by what they have actually done tonight — so by the second quarter your top backs, receivers and tacklers are the first names on the screen. Every suggestion is only a suggestion: tap to confirm, or pick anyone else.
